# Evidence and optional research tools

Research snapshot: **2026-10-19**; the two tables under “Rewriting, auditing, and feedback” were added on **2026-10-27** and every link in them was opened on that date. Later publication, release, repository, and availability statuses require rechecking against the primary sources.

This is a research-informed design, not a validated measure of human manuscript comprehension. The sources below motivate checks or identify optional tools; listing them does not install, integrate, execute, or validate them. Report actual tool use separately.

## Conceptual foundations and design inspiration

| Primary source | What to borrow and what remains unvalidated |
|---|---|
| [ReviewCritique — EMNLP 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.292/) | Borrow its attention to review deficiencies and human–model disagreement; writing disagreement in 3 of 20 directly reviewed papers does not establish a universal failure rate or the mechanism behind a particular clarity error. |
| [BooookScore — ICLR 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.00785v4) | Borrow source-localized confusion questions and error categories; its full-summary prompt permits resolution from any part of the summary, so its evaluation does not validate this skill's first-critical-use or prefix-only criterion. |
| [KnowSim — 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17150) | Borrow explicit knowledge units and prerequisites as a representation of what a reader may need; transfer from conversational knowledge-state modeling to long-manuscript review remains unvalidated. |
| [SimpleToM — ICLR 2026](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/524ff06d5375d76e93d8490e654a81bf-Abstract-Conference.html) | Borrow the distinction between identifying a knowledge boundary and applying it in subsequent reasoning; its tasks do not establish that simulated sequential reading reproduces human manuscript comprehension. |
| [Language models that match reader experience are better predictors of reading times — Journal of Memory and Language, 2026](https://sfb1102.uni-saarland.de/publication/language-models-that-match-reader-experience-are-better-predictors-of-reading-times/) | Borrow the need to specify reader experience when interpreting model-based difficulty; its short-text reading-time results do not validate a general-purpose ChatGPT or Claude manuscript reviewer. |
| [Lost in the Middle — TACL 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.tacl-1.9/) | Borrow the distinction between context availability and reliable context use; long-context retrieval results neither imply unlimited model memory nor provide a human working-memory simulator. |
| [Align to Structure — AAAI 2026](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/41085) | Borrow explicit rhetorical-structure planning as a generation design; reported task improvements do not establish better first-read comprehension of scientific papers or proposals. |
| [CogWriter — Findings of ACL 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.511/) | Borrow the separation of planning, generation, and monitoring; results on the paper's writing tasks do not validate this skill's scientific-fidelity checks or predicted reading-cost diagnoses. |
| [Why is AI-generated language so hard to understand? — 2026 preprint, arXiv:2610.29162](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29162) | Borrow the eight measurable difficulty features (object relatives with and without a relativizer, denominal verbs, inanimate subjects of animate-only verbs, standardized dependency length, stacked nouns, unattested bigrams, n-gram perplexity) as candidate-nomination heuristics, approximated in `scripts/difficulty_features.py`; the paper reports no human reading data of its own, its genres were chat, code and essays rather than academic prose, and it is a preprint, so the counts do not establish difficulty for a given reader. |

These sources provide conceptual inspiration. This skill's default review process does not execute their authors' systems, reproduce their evaluation results, or claim their published performance.

## Rewriting, auditing, and feedback: peer-reviewed sources

Each row says what the skill borrows and what the source cannot support. These sources motivated the 0.18 rules: the hedge-by-hedge fidelity audit, the tenth audit property (added qualification), scoped edit instructions with an edit-ratio threshold, separate calls for diagnosis, wording, and audit, order-swapped pairwise judgments, the excess-vocabulary nomination column, and the requirement that every reported action name a block.

| Primary source | What to borrow and what remains unvalidated |
|---|---|
| [Isch, Dörr, Fasching, Jennings et al., Quantifying the prevalence and impact of overreaching causal claims in social science — Nature Human Behaviour, 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-026-02553-x) | Borrow the finding that model summaries of abstracts drop hedges and turn associations into causal statements under a plain prompt, and that a prompt focused on methodological limits reduces it; the study measures summaries of abstracts by five named models, not sentence-level editing, and its authors note the newest model no longer shows the older one's pattern, so it does not fix a rate for any rewrite this skill audits. |
| [Thakkar et al., A large-scale randomized study of large language model feedback in peer review — Nature Machine Intelligence, 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-026-01188-x) | Borrow the result that pointing out vague comments, misreadings, and unprofessional remarks led about a quarter of ICLR reviewers to revise, with blind raters finding the revised reviews more informative; this is evidence about review comments, not about whether an edited manuscript reads better, and scores did not change. |
| [Liang et al., Can Large Language Models Provide Useful Feedback on Research Papers? — NEJM AI, 2024](https://ai.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/AIoa2400196) | Borrow the observation that model and human reviewer comments overlap about as much as two human reviewers do and that the overlap is strongest on checklist-style points, which is why `claim_check` is a list of concrete questions; the study evaluates reviews of whole papers, not wording repairs. |
| [Chamoun, Schlichtkrull and Vlachos, Automated Focused Feedback Generation for Scientific Writing Assistance — Findings of ACL 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.20477) | Borrow the design of locating a weakness before commenting on it and the human rating that specific, locatable comments are more useful; the paper evaluates feedback quality on real reviews, not the effect of the feedback on a revised text. |
| [Ou et al., CLAIMCHECK: How Grounded are LLM Critiques of Scientific Papers? — Findings of EMNLP 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.1185/) | Borrow the task split of matching a criticism to the claim it disputes and rewriting a criticism to be specific, and the result that models classify criticisms well but trail experts at grounding them; the corpus is NeurIPS reviews, so it says nothing about writing clarity or about editing. |
| [Kobak, González-Márquez, Horvát and Lause, Delving into LLM-assisted writing in biomedical publications through excess vocabulary — Science Advances, 2025](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.adt3813) | Borrow the 2024 excess style words (verbs, adjectives, adverbs) as a nomination column in `difficulty_features.py nominate` (`assets/excess-vocabulary.json` records the selection); the authors state that the estimate is a lower bound and that the method cannot tell direct model use from adopted vocabulary, so a count never shows that a sentence was machine-written. |
| [How much are LLMs changing the language of academic papers after ChatGPT? — Scientometrics, 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11192-026-05601-5) | Borrow the full-text confirmation that marker words rose steeply after 2022 and more in STEM than in the humanities; frequency change in a corpus is not a property of any one manuscript. |
| [Raheja et al., CoEdIT: Text Editing by Task-Specific Instruction Tuning — Findings of EMNLP 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-emnlp.350/) | Borrow the principle of task-specific editing instructions that preserve meaning (the `edit_scope` field and the scoped wording instruction); its results belong to its own fine-tuned models and cannot be claimed for the general model this skill runs. |
| [Zeng et al., Bridging the Editing Gap in LLMs: FineEdit for Precise and Targeted Text Modifications — Findings of EMNLP 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.118/) | Borrow the framing that precise, instruction-driven edits are hard for general models and need a benchmark of their own; the benchmark is structured editing tasks, not academic prose, and its scores are not evidence about this skill. |
| [Chen et al., XtraGPT: Context-Aware and Controllable Academic Paper Revision via Human-AI Collaboration — arXiv:2505.11336, accepted at ACL 2026 per the listing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.11336) | Borrow the claim that section-level revision with context and criteria works better than a bare prompt; the results are those of a fine-tuned model on its own corpus and transfer no score to this workflow. |
| [Du and Hashimoto, Exploring sentence-level revision capabilities of LLMs in EAP writing assistance — Asian-Pacific Journal of Second and Foreign Language Education, 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40862-025-00334-z) | Borrow the finding that output quality depends on the prompt language and the draft's quality and that academic phrasebank material in the prompt helps; the study is about second-language writing support, not about manuscript review. |
| [Kamoi, Zhang, Zhang, Han and Zhang, When Can LLMs Actually Correct Their Own Mistakes? A Critical Survey of Self-Correction of LLMs — TACL, 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.tacl-1.78/) | Borrow the survey's conclusion that self-correction without an external signal is unreliable and that verification must be easier than generation for it to work (the separate audit call); a survey of reasoning tasks does not measure manuscript editing. |
| [Huang et al., Large Language Models Cannot Self-Correct Reasoning Yet — ICLR 2024](https://openreview.net/forum?id=IkmD3fKBPQ) | Borrow the result that a model asked to review and revise its own answer often makes it worse, which is why diagnosis, wording, and audit are separate calls; the tasks are reasoning benchmarks, not prose. |
| [Oh and Schuler, Transformer-Based Language Model Surprisal Predicts Human Reading Times Best with About Two Billion Training Tokens — Findings of EMNLP 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-emnlp.128/) | Borrow the caution that larger models' surprisal fits reading times worse, so a model's own difficulty score is not a reading-time proxy; the data are ordinary reading of narrative and news text. |
| [Gruteke Klein, Meiri, Shubi and Berzak, The Effect of Surprisal on Reading Times in Information Seeking and Repeated Reading — CoNLL 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.conll-1.17/) | Borrow the finding that surprisal's predictive power drops in information-seeking and repeated reading, which is how a reviewer reads a proposal, so surprisal stays a nomination signal here; the texts are short English passages, not manuscripts. |
| [Lopes Rego, Snell and Meeter, Language models outperform cloze predictability in a cognitive model of reading — PLOS Computational Biology, 2024](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1012117) | Borrow the result that language-model predictability fits eye-movement data better than cloze norms inside a reading model; the fit concerns word-level eye movements, not comprehension of an argument. |
| [Cohen, Xu, Guo, Pakhomov and Leroy, Coherence and comprehensibility: Large language models predict lay understanding of health-related content — Journal of Biomedical Informatics, 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12243615/) | Borrow the association between model-derived coherence measures and multiple-choice comprehension, which supports running a coherence tool when a paragraph-relation check is open; lay readers of health text are not expert reviewers of a manuscript. |
| [Doshi and Hauser, Generative AI enhances individual creativity but reduces the collective diversity of novel content — Science Advances, 2024](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.adn5290) | Borrow the pattern that model assistance raises individual quality while narrowing diversity across writers, a reason to flag a flatter vocabulary; the task was short fiction, not academic prose. |

## Rewriting, auditing, and feedback: preprints (not peer reviewed)

These entries are cited as motivation only. None has been through peer review as of the snapshot date, so a rule that rests on one of them alone is marked advisory in the references.

| Preprint | What to borrow and what remains unvalidated |
|---|---|
| [From “May” to “Is”: Certainty Distortion in Language Model Rewriting — arXiv:2606.07951](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07951) | Borrow the measured direction and accumulation of certainty change in meaning-preserving rewrites (strengthening more often than weakening, growing over rounds), the reason for the hedge-by-hedge audit and per-round auditing; measured on scientific and medical passages with a certainty metric, not on a review workflow. |
| [Defensive Writing in GPT Models — arXiv:2610.11355](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11355) | Borrow the opposite drift: unsupported qualifiers added when a model is asked to check claims or told about reviewer concerns, the reason for the `added_qualification` property and for prompts that ask what the text says rather than for a reviewer's stance; one preprint with a small reader study. |
| [Jourdan, Boudin, Dufour and Hernandez, Identifying Reliable Evaluation Metrics for Scientific Text Revision — arXiv:2506.04772 (the listing names ACL 2025 main; cited here as the arXiv version)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.04772) | Borrow the measured weakness of model judges on correctness versus instruction-following and the low human agreement on preference (κ 0.33), the reason for order-swapped pairwise judgments and for describing differences instead of grading; NLP paper paragraphs with non-native annotators. |
| [KnowledgeGain: Evaluating and Optimizing Science News Generation for Reader Learning — arXiv:2605.31099](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.31099) | Borrow the memory-trace reader design (answer from notes only, with an I-don't-know option): the masked reading mode's recall events (masked-reading.md 5b) and the prefix reader's `cannot_place` list adopt it; science news and undergraduate readers, with a controlled human experiment but no peer review. |
| [HyperEdit — arXiv:2512.12544](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.12544) | Borrow the measurement of over-editing by general models; a benchmark preprint, no transfer of scores. |
| [How LLMs Distort Our Written Language — arXiv:2603.18161](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18161) | Borrow the observation that even grammar-only requests change meaning and that mixed review-and-rewrite tasks amplify it, the reason for separate calls; a preprint whose review-corpus estimates are unverified. |
| [The Self-Correction Illusion — arXiv:2606.05976](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05976) | Borrow the finding that the same text presented as someone else's is corrected far more often, the reason the audit prompt says the rewrite was submitted by someone else; one preprint. |
| [Wataoka et al., Self-Preference Bias in LLM-as-a-Judge — arXiv:2410.21819](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21819) and [Justice or Prejudice? — arXiv:2410.02736](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02736) | Borrow the documented judge biases toward low-perplexity, longer, and first-listed candidates; both are preprints about general judging, not manuscript review. |
| [Human–LLM Coevolution: Evidence from Academic Writing — arXiv:2502.09606](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09606) and [How Did Writing Change at CHI? — arXiv:2609.23090](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23090) | Borrow the evidence that marker words fell back after being named, the reason the excess-vocabulary list is dated and advisory; corpus studies without peer review. |
| [What Are LLMs Doing to Scientific Communication? — arXiv:2605.19936](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.19936) | Borrow the measured longer words and lower lexical diversity after model rewriting (the type-token and word-length flags in `compare`) and the small expert pilot that preferred the rewrites (why “sounding less like a model” is never a goal); one preprint. |
| [JRE-L — arXiv:2501.16865](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.16865) | Borrow the design of a note-taking reader model instead of a direct readability score; one preprint. |

## Optional tools for direct use after separate setup

| Primary project source | Appropriate direct use and limit |
|---|---|
| [TAACO — authors' repository](https://github.com/LCR-ADS-Lab/TAACO) | If separately installed and tested, use its cohesion indices to nominate passages for inspection when a paragraph-relation check is open (Cohen et al. 2024 above found coherence measures associated with lay comprehension); lexical and semantic connections do not establish prerequisite order, valid inference, or human comprehension. |
| [Comprehensive Coherence Calculator (CCC) — authors' repository](https://github.com/WeizheXu/coherencepip) | If separately installed and tested, use its semantic-relatedness measures as supplementary signals; such scores do not determine whether a necessary definition arrives before critical use or whether evidence supports a claim. |

Before any optional execution, verify current installation instructions, model/data dependencies, license, processing location, and the exact operation requested; avoid adding a dependency unless it resolves a concrete review need.

## Interpretation rules

- Separate direct empirical findings, task-transfer hypotheses, and this skill's design choices in explanations to the user.
- Do not cite an adjacent benchmark as validation of the complete review workflow.
- Describe generated findings as model-based predictions unless actual reader observations were provided.
- Distinguish a runnable repository, a paper with project links, and an integration that has actually been executed successfully.
- Evaluating the full workflow requires human comprehension and retrieval-cost outcomes, false-positive analysis, and checks that edits preserve scientific meaning.
